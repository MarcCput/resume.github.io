Overall Reflection

How my five artefacts connect, and what the whole project taught me.

The brief describes four steps for an e-portfolio: collect the artefacts, select the best evidence, reflect on it, and connect the pieces into one summary. The five component pages hold the first three steps. This page is the fourth: it shows how the artefacts fit together and what I learned across the whole project.

Collect, select, reflect, connect

Collect

I gathered the team charter (4 May 2026), my stand-up logbook (April to May and 28 to 31 July), the user stories and tasks, the product backlog, 52 sprint cards from 14 July to 9 October 2026, and my GitHub commit history.

Select

I chose five artefacts because each answers a different question: who we are and how we work (charter), what users need (stories), what comes first (product backlog), who does what and when (sprint backlog), and what happened day to day (logbook).

Each component page ends with my reflection on what I did, why, what I learned, the challenges, the contribution and what I would improve.

What the whole project taught me

  1. Planning records only help when they are true. On 29 October 2026 I compared the product backlog with the repository. Seven items were ticked complete on the board but were not yet in the code. I built them the same day, recorded the date and the known limits, and said plainly that they were delivered after the sprint record ended. From now on I check the board against the code, and I record the real date when a feature is late.
  2. Accept feedback early and raise blockers quickly. The lecturer's feedback on 28 July showed that our backlog did not follow Scrum, and finding that before development saved rework. The blocker that kept returning was waiting for confirmation from team members.
  3. Order work by dependency. We agreed the order as design, then architecture, backend, frontend and testing, because later work depends on earlier work.
  4. A role can grow. The charter describes my role as Designer. During development I also built backend and frontend features, and I added my name to those cards to keep the sprint record honest.
  5. Be open about tools. I built the platform with Claude, an AI coding assistant. I decided the features, the structure and what got committed, and I say so on the Product Backlog page because the commits list Claude as co-author.

Is the Sprint 29 to 32 work finished?

Yes. The backend and database tasks were completed and shown to our supervisor on 16 September 2026 (Sprint 38). We found and fixed permission bugs along the way, including students changing their own document status and providers changing their own ratings. Row-level security is on for all 11 tables in the database, and passwords are stored only as hashes by Supabase Auth.

What I would do differently

  • Set a due date for every confirmation the team needs, so waiting does not hold up a sprint.
  • Write a goal at the start of each sprint week, and hold a short review and retrospective at the end.
  • Keep a stand-up note for every working day, not only for some weeks.
  • Keep acceptance criteria on every backlog item from the start, and tick an item off only when the work is in the code and has been tested.
  • Review the team charter at the start of each phase.
