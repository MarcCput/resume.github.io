Agile Team Charter

Our living social contract: shared purpose, values, working agreements and operational boundaries.

Section 1: Evidence

The NavigSA Agile Team Charter was written by the whole team and dated 4 May 2026. It is a 13-page document that defines the team's purpose, roles, values, communication plan, goals, working agreements, sprint structure and sign-off. I summarise each part below and show what it demonstrates.

Purpose and vision

Objective: to design and validate a business concept that addresses the difficulty international students from non-English-speaking countries face with SAQA evaluation, university applications, study visas and accommodation.

"To be the most trusted navigator for international students entering South Africa's tertiary education system, removing barriers of language, confusion, and exploitation through affordable, multilingual, and step-by-step guidance."

Values and Agile principles

Core values

  • Collaboration: every member contributes and decisions are shared.
  • Respect: we honour each other's time, opinions and culture.
  • Transparency: no surprises at submission time.
  • Accountability: members own their tasks.
  • Excellence and Empathy for the people we research.

Agile principles we follow

  • Individuals and interactions over processes and tools
  • Working deliverables over comprehensive documentation
  • Collaborating with our community through validation interviews
  • Responding to change based on feedback
  • Continuous improvement after each milestone

Communication and decision-making

ChannelPurposeFrequency
WhatsApp groupDaily check-ins, quick updates, sharing linksAs needed
Microsoft TeamsSharing work and getting feedbackWeekly (minimum)
In-person meetingsSprint planning, task allocation, progress review, problem-solvingWeekly (minimum)
EmailFormal communication with the lecturer and external peopleThroughout the project
  • Meetings are scheduled at least 48 hours ahead, with an agenda sent on WhatsApp.
  • One member takes notes and shares a summary within 24 hours. Decisions are logged in the shared Google Doc.
  • Absent members read the summary and confirm within 24 hours.
  • Decision-making: routine decisions by majority agreement in the team chat; major decisions (for example changing the business idea) need a full team discussion; the Team Leader has the casting vote in a deadlock.

Working agreements, accountability and conflict resolution

  • Work goes into the shared folder at least 24 hours before the group deadline.
  • Nothing is submitted unless every member has reviewed and approved it.
  • A member who cannot finish a task must tell the team at least 48 hours ahead so the work can be redistributed (accountability).
  • Lecturer feedback is reviewed together and turned into tasks in the next sprint planning session.
  • Credit is shared openly and we do not claim each other's contributions.
  • Conflict resolution: conflicts are settled directly between the people involved first. If they are unresolved, the Team Leader mediates.
  • We keep to academic integrity and submit our own work.

What this evidence demonstrates

The charter covers each element the brief describes: a shared purpose (vision statement), core values, working agreements and operational boundaries (deadlines, review rules, notice periods). It also sets out accountability and conflict-resolution steps, which are what reduce friction in a team.

Section 2: Personal reflection

1. What I did and what the artefact represents

I took part in the NavigSA team charter as the team's Designer, and all four of us signed it on 4 May 2026. The charter is our agreement on purpose, roles, values, communication and working rules. I then wrote my own individual charter for the Designer role (27 October 2026). In it I set my purpose ("to design a clear, consistent, and user-friendly visual experience that makes NavigSA easy to understand and trust"), my responsibilities and my SMART goals: initial design concepts and branding by Week 3, consistent visuals by Week 4 and final design assets by Week 5.

2. Why I approached it this way

I wrote my charter around what a Designer owes the team: visual identity, the prototype and user flow, slides and layouts, and designs that are simple for non-native English speakers. This matches my April and May logbook entries, where I researched the SAQA process, planned the user journey and created wireframes. I gave every goal a week so that the team could check my work against it. I also used weekly milestones (Weeks 3, 4 and 5) that match our sprint plan, so that each goal could be checked against the work.

3. What I learned

I learned that a charter only works when it shows up in daily habits. Our values of transparency and accountability became real through the daily stand-ups and the Trello board, where every task had a name and a status. My main lessons from the first sprint planning week were to accept feedback early, keep the board accurate and raise blockers quickly.

4. Challenges and how I addressed them

The challenge that kept returning in my July logbook was waiting for confirmation from other team members before work could be finalised (29 to 31 July 2026). The charter already has a rule that members must give 48 hours' notice if they cannot finish a task, but a rule about late work does not help when a decision is needed from everyone. I raised the delays in our stand-ups and followed up with members directly. We reviewed the backlog together on 30 July and finalised the first development sprint on 31 July. Next time I would set a due date for each person's confirmation.

5. How it contributed to the project

The communication plan (WhatsApp, Teams, weekly meetings and later Trello) and the sprint approach in the charter gave us the structure we used for planning and tracking. My own contribution was the visual side: the NavigSA identity, the layouts and slides, and the Figma prototype that I presented to the lecturer and class on 28 July 2026.

6. What I would improve

The team charter was written for the first phase and describes six sprint weeks, but the development phase later ran for 13 sprint weeks. I would review the charter at the start of each phase and update it. I would also agree a specific due date for every confirmation the team needs, so that waiting does not hold up a sprint.
